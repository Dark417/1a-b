# 11 · Training & alignment — data, scaling laws, tokenization, RLHF/DPO, long context

> **Intuition.** An LLM's behavior is set by *what* it's trained on (data), *how
> big and how long* (scaling laws), *how text becomes tokens* (tokenization), and
> *how it's shaped to be useful and safe afterwards* (alignment). This file is the
> training-side companion to the architecture explainers.

---

## 1. Pretraining data

The objective is plain next-token (or span) prediction, but **data dominates
quality**. The web-scale corpora that recur: **Common Crawl → C4 / RefinedWeb /
FineWeb** (filtered web), **The Pile / Dolma** (open mixes), code (GitHub/The
Stack), books, Wikipedia, math, and increasingly **synthetic** data (Phi's
"textbooks," distillation). Modern runs use **10–18T tokens** (LLaMA-3 15T,
Qwen2.5 18T, DeepSeek-V3 14.8T). Filtering, dedup, and mixture weights matter as
much as size; **OLMo/Dolma** and **Pythia** exist to make this reproducible.

---

## 2. Scaling laws

- **Kaplan et al. (2020).** Test loss falls as a **power law** in parameters $N$,
  data $D$, and compute $C$. Their fits suggested spending most of a bigger budget
  on **more parameters**.
- **Chinchilla / Hoffmann et al. (2022).** Corrected this: for compute
  $C \approx 6ND$, the optimum scales $N$ and $D$ **together** — roughly
  **20 tokens/parameter**. Chinchilla-70B beat Gopher-280B. (See
  `07.google-models.md`.)
- **Inference-aware over-training.** Chinchilla optimizes *training* cost only.
  For models you'll serve a lot, **over-train a smaller model** (LLaMA-3-8B on
  15T ≈ 1875 tok/param) so inference is cheap. The compute-optimal point and the
  *deployment*-optimal point differ.
- **New scaling axis — inference compute.** o1/o3 and R1 show accuracy scales with
  **test-time reasoning tokens** (`03.gpt-line.md`, `06.qwen-deepseek.md`), not
  just train-time scale.

$$L(N,D) \approx E + \frac{A}{N^{\alpha}} + \frac{B}{D^{\beta}} \qquad (\text{Chinchilla form})$$

## 3. Tokenization

Text → integers via subword algorithms:
- **BPE (Byte-Pair Encoding).** Greedily merge the most frequent symbol pairs.
  GPT-2/3, LLaMA-3 (**tiktoken**, byte-level, 128k vocab), Mistral.
- **WordPiece.** Likelihood-based merges — BERT.
- **SentencePiece / Unigram.** Language-agnostic, trains on raw bytes (no
  pre-tokenization) — T5, LLaMA-1/2, many multilingual models.
- **Byte-level fallback** guarantees any string is encodable. Bigger vocab =
  fewer tokens/word (cheaper long context, better code/multilingual) but a larger
  embedding matrix — hence the move from 32k → 128k+ vocabularies.

## 4. Alignment — turning a base LM into an assistant

### Instruction tuning (SFT)
Supervised fine-tune on (instruction, good response) pairs so the model follows
instructions and adopts a chat format. **Flan** (1.8k tasks) and self-generated
instruction sets (Alpaca, etc.) are canonical. Cheap, high-leverage; the
foundation every chat model starts from.

### RLHF — Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback
The InstructGPT recipe (`03.gpt-line.md`):
1. **SFT** an initial policy.
2. Collect human **preference rankings**; train a **reward model** $r_\phi$.
3. Optimize the policy with **PPO** to maximize reward **minus a KL penalty** to
   the SFT reference (so it doesn't drift into gibberish / reward hacking):

$$\max_\theta\ \mathbb{E}_{x,y\sim\pi_\theta}\big[r_\phi(x,y)\big] - \beta\,\mathrm{KL}\!\big(\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)\,\|\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot|x)\big)$$

### DPO — Direct Preference Optimization
Skip the explicit reward model and RL loop: a closed-form result shows you can
optimize the policy **directly on preference pairs** $(y_w \succ y_l)$ with a
simple classification-style loss, where the implicit reward is
$\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\text{ref}}}$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}} = -\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\log\sigma\big(\beta\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w|x)} - \beta\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l|x)}\big)\Big]$$

Simpler and more stable than PPO; now the default for many open models.
Relatives: **IPO, KTO, ORPO, SimPO**.

### RLAIF and verifiable-reward RL
- **RLAIF** replaces human labels with an **AI judge** (e.g. Constitutional AI:
  the model critiques/revises itself against principles) — scales preference data.
- **GRPO / verifiable rewards** (DeepSeek-R1, `06.qwen-deepseek.md`): for math/code
  use an *automatic* correctness reward and **group-relative** advantages (no
  critic network) to train **reasoning**.

## 5. MoE training concerns

Routing can **collapse** (a few experts get all tokens). Fixes (`07.moe.py`):
- **Load-balancing auxiliary loss** (Switch/Mixtral) encouraging uniform usage.
- **Capacity factor** + token dropping/overflow handling.
- **Aux-loss-free balancing** (DeepSeek-V3): a learned per-expert routing **bias**
  nudged to equalize load without a competing loss.
- **Router z-loss** for logit stability; **expert/tensor parallelism** for scale.

## 6. Long context (extending the window)

Models train at one length and **extend** at/after training, almost always by
manipulating **RoPE** (`01.rope.py`):
- **Position Interpolation (PI).** Divide positions by a scale $s = L_{\text{new}}/L_{\text{train}}$ to squeeze new positions into the trained range; hurts local detail.
- **NTK-aware scaling.** Scale the RoPE **base** instead of positions — stretches
  low frequencies, preserves high-frequency (local) resolution.
- **YaRN.** Interpolate **per-wavelength** (no interpolation for short wavelengths,
  full for long, a ramp between) + an attention **temperature** to keep softmax
  entropy stable. Best quality; used by Qwen, others to reach 128k+.
- Architectural long-context: **sliding-window** (`09.sliding_window.py`), **MLA**
  KV compression (`04.mla.py`), and **SSM/hybrid** linear-time models
  (`10.non-transformer.md`).
