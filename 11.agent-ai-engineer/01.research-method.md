# 01 · Research Method

How the ranked AI-engineer skill profile in this section was built: where the
data came from, how big the sample was, how skills were tallied/weighted/ranked,
and what the limitations are. The goal is **transparency** — you should be able
to disagree with a ranking and trace exactly why it landed where it did.

> **Date of research:** June 2026. The job market for AI engineers moves fast;
> treat frequencies as a snapshot, not a constant. The *trend* column in
> [`04.skill-frequency.md`](04.skill-frequency.md) flags what is rising/falling.

---

## 1. What question we were answering

> *"For a job titled 'AI Engineer' / 'LLM Engineer' / 'ML Engineer (GenAI)' /
> 'Research Engineer' / 'Forward-Deployed / Applied-AI Engineer' / 'Agent
> Engineer' in 2025–2026, which skills and knowledge areas do employers
> actually ask for, and how often?"*

We deliberately cast a **wide net over role titles** because the market has not
standardized: the same underlying skill set is advertised under many names. We
treat them as one population (the "AI engineer" archetype) and note where a
sub-archetype (e.g. *research engineer* vs *forward-deployed engineer*) skews the
demand.

## 3. Sample size

- **30 primary job-description sources (S1–S30)** spanning **~20 distinct
  employers** — frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind, Mistral), big tech
  (Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, NVIDIA, Netflix), AI-platform companies (Databricks,
  Scale, Perplexity, Palantir, Salesforce), and startups (YC/Sei + the HN
  "Who is hiring?" and YC aggregate boards, which themselves cover dozens of
  seed/early-stage postings).
- **12 market/trend reports (R1–R12)** for calibration, two of which (R1, R2)
  publish explicit per-skill frequency percentages.
- Role archetypes covered: **research engineer, applied/forward-deployed
  engineer, LLM/ML engineer, agent engineer, inference/performance engineer,
  distributed-training engineer, MLOps/LLMOps engineer, applied scientist.**

This is a **medium-sized, breadth-first** sample. It is large enough to see which
skills are universal vs niche, but it is **not** a statistically rigorous random
sample of all postings — see Limitations.

## 4. How skills were extracted

1. For each source, we read the **condensed requirement summary** (we did *not*
   copy verbatim JD text — we extracted and paraphrased the skills, per the house
   citation rules and to respect copyright).
2. Each requirement was mapped to a **canonical skill name** from a controlled
   vocabulary (e.g. "knows LangGraph/CrewAI/AutoGen" → *Agent frameworks &
   orchestration*; "LoRA/QLoRA/RLHF" → *Fine-tuning & alignment*). This
   normalization is what makes counting meaningful — raw phrasing varies wildly.
3. Skills were bucketed into ~30 canonical areas (the list that became
   [`agent-ai-engineer.skill.md`](agent-ai-engineer.skill.md)).

## 5. How we tallied, weighted, and ranked

The final rank is a **weighted demand score**, not a raw count, computed per
canonical skill:

```
score(skill) =  Σ_over_sources  mention(skill, source) × W_role × W_seniority
              + calibration_adjustment(skill)        # from R1/R2 % where available
```

- **mention** = 1 if the skill appears as a required/desired item in that source
  (required counts full; nice-to-have counts half).
- **W_role** — a light weight so we don't let one over-represented archetype
  dominate. Generalist "AI/LLM/ML engineer" roles weight 1.0; specialist roles
  (inference, distributed training) weight 0.8 for skills *specific* to them, so
  CUDA doesn't outrank Python just because we happened to sample NVIDIA twice.
- **W_seniority** — senior/staff/principal asks weight 1.2 (they signal what the
  market values for ownership: system design, evaluation, communication);
  new-grad asks weight 0.9.
- **calibration_adjustment** — where a market report (R1/R2) gives a hard
  percentage, we nudge the rank toward it. Example: R1 reports PyTorch at 37.7%
  of postings and NLP at 19.7%, which confirms PyTorch/DL foundations outrank any
  single framework.

Skills were then sorted by score and **grouped into three tiers**:

- **Tier 1 — Core (table stakes):** appeared in the large majority of sources and
  in essentially every archetype. Absence is disqualifying.
- **Tier 2 — Important (strong demand):** appeared in roughly half of sources or
  is universal within a major archetype.
- **Tier 3 — Differentiating (edge / niche / seniority signal):** raises a
  candidate above the bar or is required only for specialist roles.

The tiering is **judgment applied on top of the score**, informed by reading the
sources holistically (a skill mentioned slightly less often but described as
"non-negotiable" was promoted). This is stated plainly so a reader can contest it.

## 6. Limitations & biases (read this)

- **Not a random sample.** Sources were chosen for *breadth across employer type
  and role archetype*, then supplemented by aggregate boards. Frontier labs and
  US companies are over-represented relative to the true global population of
  AI-engineering jobs.
- **Search-summary distillation.** Several sources were read through web-search
  result summaries / aggregator pages rather than the raw JD, because some
  careers pages (Greenhouse index, metacareers) render only as a job *index*
  through automated fetching. We never invented requirements; where a JD's detail
  was thin we relied on the employer's own role family description and flagged
  report-derived numbers as report-derived.
- **Title soup.** "AI engineer," "LLM engineer," "ML engineer (GenAI)," "research
  engineer," and "applied scientist" overlap but are not identical. We merged them
  on purpose; this inflates the apparent universality of skills common to all
  (Python, ML foundations) and dilutes archetype-specific ones (CUDA, RLHF).
- **Recency skew.** Agent/agentic and evaluation skills are *rising fast*; a
  snapshot under-weights them relative to where they'll be in 12 months. The
  trend column compensates qualitatively.
- **Self-reported by employers.** A JD is a wishlist, not a measurement of what
  the hire actually does day to day. Treat "required" as aspirational.
- **Searches that came up thin.** Direct fetches of Greenhouse/metacareers index
  pages returned only titles, not full descriptions; those employers are still
  represented via their role-family text and corroborating reports. Where we lean
  on well-known industry knowledge to fill a gap (e.g. that "system design" is
  expected at senior level even when a JD is terse), it is **flagged inline** as
  such in [`02.job-descriptions.md`](02.job-descriptions.md).

## 7. Reproducing / extending this

To refresh: re-run the searches behind S1–S30 and R1–R12, re-extract to the
canonical vocabulary in [`agent-ai-engineer.skill.md`](agent-ai-engineer.skill.md),
re-tally with the formula in §5, and update
[`04.skill-frequency.md`](04.skill-frequency.md). Keep the skill *names* stable —
they seed [section 12](../12.agent-ai-skills/), so renames cascade.
