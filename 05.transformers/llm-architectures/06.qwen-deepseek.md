# 06 · Qwen & DeepSeek — MLA, fine-grained MoE, MTP, and RL reasoning

> **Intuition.** The Chinese frontier labs pushed open models past the LLaMA
> template on three fronts: **cheaper attention memory** (DeepSeek's *Multi-head
> Latent Attention*), **better sparsity** (fine-grained + shared experts with
> load-balancing that needs no aux loss), and **richer training signals**
> (multi-token prediction; RL that teaches reasoning). DeepSeek-V3/R1 showed a
> 671B-total MoE could be trained at a fraction of frontier cost and rival the
> closed models.

---

## 1. Qwen (Alibaba)

A broad open family (0.5B → 110B) with strong multilingual (esp. Chinese) and
code/math. Same modern backbone as LLaMA (RMSNorm, RoPE, SwiGLU, GQA) plus:

- **Qwen1.5 → Qwen2 → Qwen2.5.** Qwen2.5 (Sept 2024) trained on **18T tokens**,
  sizes 0.5/1.5/3/7/14/32/72B, context **128k** (via YaRN RoPE scaling), 8k
  generation. Most sizes Apache-2.0 (3B/72B under Qwen licenses). Specialist
  spin-offs: **Qwen2.5-Coder**, **Qwen2.5-Math**.
- **Qwen-MoE** (e.g. Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B): **14.3B total / 2.7B active** using
  **fine-grained experts + shared experts** (see §3) — an early open MoE that
  matched a 7B dense model at ~⅓ the active compute.
- **Qwen2-VL / Qwen2.5-VL** — the vision branch (covered in `09.multimodal.md`):
  dynamic-resolution patches and **M-RoPE** (multimodal rotary positions).

Qwen's value is breadth + permissive licensing + reliably near-SOTA open quality.

## 2. DeepSeek — the architecture that turned heads

### Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) — `04.mla.py`
GQA shrinks the KV cache by *sharing* KV heads. **MLA** instead **compresses**
the keys and values into a **low-rank latent vector** that is cached; the
per-head K and V are reconstructed on the fly by up-projection. You cache a small
latent $c^{KV}_t$ (and a small decoupled RoPE key) instead of full K/V:

$$c^{KV}_t = x_t W^{DKV}\ \ (\text{cached, small}),\qquad k^C_t = c^{KV}_t W^{UK},\quad v_t = c^{KV}_t W^{UV}$$

A separate small RoPE-carrying key handles position (decoupled so the compression
doesn't fight rotary). Net effect: **KV cache far smaller than GQA** with quality
*better* than full multi-head attention — the headline efficiency win of
DeepSeek-V2/V3. See `04.mla.py` and contrast with `03.gqa_mqa.py` / `08.kv_cache.py`.

### DeepSeekMoE — fine-grained + shared experts — `07.moe.py`
Two refinements over Mixtral-style MoE:
1. **Fine-grained experts.** Split each expert into many *smaller* experts and
   route to more of them (e.g. top-k of a large pool). More combinations →
   better specialization at the same active-param budget.
2. **Shared (always-on) experts.** A few experts every token uses, capturing
   common knowledge, so the routed experts can specialize without redundancy.

### Auxiliary-loss-free load balancing (V3)
Classic MoE adds a balancing **aux loss** that fights the language loss. V3
instead adds a **learned per-expert bias** to routing scores, nudged up/down to
equalize load — balancing **without** a competing loss term, improving quality.

### Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) — `14.mtp.py`
Train the model to predict **several future tokens** (not just the next one) via
small sequential MTP heads. This densifies the training signal and improves the
main model; at inference the extra heads can drive **speculative decoding**
(`11.speculative_decoding.py`) for speedups.

### The models
- **DeepSeek-V2 (2024):** 236B total / **21B active**, MLA + DeepSeekMoE,
  128k context.
- **DeepSeek-V3 (Dec 2024):** **671B total / 37B active**, MLA + DeepSeekMoE +
  aux-loss-free balancing + **MTP**, trained on **14.8T tokens** with FP8 mixed
  precision at famously low cost; 128k context. Rivals GPT-4o-class quality open.
- **DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025):** *same architecture as V3-Base*, but post-trained
  with **RL for reasoning** (§3 below). MIT-licensed, with distilled smaller
  variants.

## 3. R1 and GRPO — teaching reasoning with RL

**R1-Zero** showed you can elicit long chain-of-thought **with pure RL** (no SFT)
using a simple, *verifiable* reward (correct answer / proper format) — the model
spontaneously learns to "think longer." **R1** adds a small cold-start SFT +
multi-stage RL for readability. The RL algorithm is **GRPO (Group-Relative Policy
Optimization)**: sample a *group* of responses per prompt, use the group's mean
reward as the baseline (so **no separate critic/value network**), and push each
response's probability up or down by its **advantage** relative to the group:

$$A_i = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(r_1{:}r_G)}{\text{std}(r_1{:}r_G)}$$

Cheaper and more scalable than PPO for this setting. (More on RLHF/PPO/DPO/GRPO
in `11.training-and-alignment.md`.)

## 4. Strengths / limits

Strengths: MLA gives long context at low KV memory; fine-grained+shared MoE gives
strong quality per active FLOP; MTP and GRPO are reusable training tricks; weights
are open (R1 is MIT). Limits: MoE serving needs lots of memory for all experts;
MLA adds implementation complexity; the very large totals (671B) need real
clusters even though active compute is modest.
