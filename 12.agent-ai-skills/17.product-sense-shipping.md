# 17 · Product Sense & Shipping

> **Scope (from the ranked list):** judgment about *what* to build — scoping an
> MVP, prioritizing by user value, iterating from real usage, pragmatic
> tradeoffs, and actually shipping. *~45% of sources; strong at startups/FDE.*

---

## 1. What it is

**Product sense** is the judgment that decides *which* problem to solve, *how
much* to build, and *when* it is good enough to ship. **Shipping** is the
discipline of actually getting that to real users, fast, and then improving it
from what they do.

For an AI engineer this is not "soft skills." It is a hard, learnable skill with
a measurable output: features that users keep using and that move a metric.
LLMs have made *capability* cheap — a weekend hackathon can demo almost
anything. What stays scarce is turning capability into a product that survives
contact with real users, edge cases, latency budgets, cost ceilings, and the
non-determinism of generative models. That gap is exactly what "product sense &
shipping" names.

It exists as a named hiring skill because the failure mode of AI teams in
2023–2026 was *not* "we couldn't build it." It was "we built an impressive demo
that never shipped, or shipped and nobody used it." Product sense is the
antidote: ruthless scoping, a tight build–measure–learn loop, and a bias to
ship.

## 2. Why employers want it

Section 11 ranks this #17 (*~45%*), and notes it is **strongest at startups and
Forward-Deployed-Engineer (FDE) roles** — Palantir/Salesforce/Anthropic-style
roles where you sit with a customer and turn fuzzy needs into a working system.
The rationale (verbatim sense): *"AI capability is cheap; turning it into a
product users keep is the scarce skill."*

Concretely, employers screen for it because:

- **Demos don't pay rent.** A model is a `model.generate()` call; the value is
  the workflow around it. Teams over-index on model quality and under-index on
  whether anyone needs the output.
- **AI scope is unbounded.** Generative systems can do *anything badly*. Without
  scoping discipline, projects sprawl and never ship.
- **Iteration speed wins.** The team that ships a narrow v1 and learns from real
  traffic beats the team perfecting a broad v1 in a doc.
- **Evidence of shipping is hireable.** FDE/startup interviews ask for *deployed*
  work — a live agent, a repo with users, a metric you moved — not coursework.

It cross-cuts every other skill in this section: evaluation (#07) tells you *if*
it's good; observability (#22) tells you *how it's used*; system design (#13)
tells you *how to build it*; product sense tells you *whether to build it at
all* and *when to stop polishing*.

## 3. Mental model

Think in three nested loops, fastest-innermost:

```
   ┌─────────────── Strategy loop (months) ───────────────┐
   │  Which problem? Who is the user? What's the wedge?    │
   │   ┌───────────── Product loop (weeks) ───────────┐    │
   │   │  Scope MVP → ship → measure adoption/quality │    │
   │   │   ┌──────── Build loop (days) ───────────┐   │    │
   │   │   │  prompt/eval/iterate → demo → cut     │   │    │
   │   │   └──────────────────────────────────────┘   │    │
   │   └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘    │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Core frameworks worth internalizing (in words):**

- **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD).** Users "hire" a product to make progress in a
  situation. Ask "what job is this being hired for?" not "what feature do they
  want?" An LLM summarizer might be hired for *"let me skip reading"* — so speed
  and trust matter more than eloquence.
- **MVP = riskiest assumption, tested cheapest.** The MVP is not "v1 with fewer
  features." It is the smallest thing that *validates the single most uncertain
  belief*. If the risk is "will users trust an AI answer?", the MVP might be a
  Slack bot with a human in the loop, not a polished web app.
- **Build–Measure–Learn (Lean Startup).** Every build choice should produce a
  *learning*. If you can't name what you'll learn, you're gold-plating.
- **Prioritization scoring.** Two common rubrics:
  - **RICE** = `Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort`. Rank features by score.
  - **ICE** = `Impact × Confidence × Ease` (lighter weight).
- **The AI-specific twist — the "jagged frontier."** LLM capability is *uneven*:
  trivially does X, fails at near-identical Y. Product sense for AI means
  *probing where the frontier is jagged for your use case* before you commit a
  roadmap to it. Always build a 20-example eval set of *real* inputs first.
- **Cost/latency/quality is a triangle.** You usually pick two. A product
  decision (not a model decision) sets which corner you sacrifice: a coding
  agent trades latency for quality; a chat autocomplete trades quality for
  latency.

**The shipping checklist (the part people skip):** auth, error states,
rate-limit handling, graceful degradation when the model is wrong, a feedback
capture mechanism (👍/👎), logging/observability, and a kill switch. A feature
isn't shipped until it survives a bad model day.

## 4. The landscape

Tools and techniques, organized by where they help in the loop.

**Discovery & scoping**
- **JTBD interviews / customer discovery** (Mom Test by Rob Fitzpatrick): ask
  about past behavior, not hypothetical wants.
- **Opportunity Solution Trees** (Teresa Torres): map outcome → opportunities →
  solutions → experiments. Keeps you anchored to an outcome metric.
- **PRD / one-pager / design doc:** a tight spec (problem, user, success metric,
  non-goals, risks). *Non-goals* are the most valuable section for AI scope
  control.

**Prioritization**
- **RICE / ICE** scoring (above). **Kano model** (must-haves vs delighters).
  **MoSCoW** (Must/Should/Could/Won't). **Cost of Delay / WSJF** for sequencing.

**Build & validate fast**
- **No/low-code prototyping:** Streamlit, Gradio (HF Spaces), Retool, v0,
  Replit — get a clickable thing in hours.
- **Wizard-of-Oz / concierge MVP:** a human pretends to be the AI to test demand
  before building it.
- **Feature flags / staged rollout:** LaunchDarkly, Statsig, Unleach,
  GrowthBook — ship to 1% first.

**Measure**
- **Product analytics:** PostHog, Amplitude, Mixpanel, Statsig — funnels,
  retention, activation.
- **AI-specific:** LLM observability (#22 — Langfuse/LangSmith/Helicone) for
  per-feature cost, latency, and thumbs-up rate; an eval set (#07) for quality
  regressions.
- **The metrics that matter:** *activation* (did they reach the aha?),
  *retention* (do they come back?), *task success rate* (AI-specific: did the
  agent actually complete the job?), and **North-Star** (the one metric that
  proxies value, e.g. "accepted suggestions / day").

**Experiment**
- **A/B testing** infrastructure (see #31 for the statistics), feature
  experiments, prompt/model A/Bs (route 50% to a new prompt, compare task
  success).

**Tradeoffs to hold in your head**
- *Polish vs. ship:* every extra week of polish is a week of lost learning.
- *General vs. narrow:* a narrow tool that nails one job beats a broad assistant
  that does everything mediocrely (the "do-anything chatbot" trap).
- *Automation vs. human-in-the-loop:* HITL ships faster and de-risks trust; full
  automation scales but raises the quality bar to where errors are acceptable.

## 5. Worked example — scoping & gating an AI feature

A lightweight, idiomatic Python harness an engineer can actually run to make a
*shipping decision*: RICE-rank candidate features, then gate the chosen one on a
real eval before committing. No external services; runs on CPU instantly.

```python
"""ship_decide.py — prioritize AI features (RICE) and gate the winner on an eval.
Run: python ship_decide.py
"""
from dataclasses import dataclass


@dataclass
class Feature:
    name: str
    reach: float        # users/quarter touched
    impact: float       # 3=massive,2=high,1=med,.5=low,.25=minimal
    confidence: float   # 0..1 (evidence we have)
    effort: float       # person-weeks

    @property
    def rice(self) -> float:
        return self.reach * self.impact * self.confidence / self.effort


def prioritize(features: list[Feature]) -> list[Feature]:
    return sorted(features, key=lambda f: f.rice, reverse=True)


# --- A tiny REAL eval set: the riskiest assumption is "the model can do the task" ---
# Here: an "extract the invoice total" feature. Build the eval BEFORE the roadmap.
GOLD = [
    ("Total due: $1,240.00", "1240.00"),
    ("Amount payable EUR 89,90", "89.90"),     # comma decimal — a jagged-frontier case
    ("Balance: 0", "0.00"),
    ("TOTAL  ......  $3,499.99 USD", "3499.99"),
]


def fake_model(text: str) -> str:
    """Stand-in for an LLM call. In real life: anthropic/openai client.
    Deliberately fails the comma-decimal case to show the eval catching it."""
    import re
    m = re.search(r"[\d][\d,]*\.\d{2}|\b\d+\b", text)
    raw = m.group(0).replace(",", "") if m else "ERR"
    try:
        return f"{float(raw):.2f}"
    except ValueError:
        return "ERR"


def eval_pass_rate(model) -> float:
    correct = sum(model(t) == y for t, y in GOLD)
    return correct / len(GOLD)


def ship_decision(rate: float, bar: float = 0.85) -> str:
    return "SHIP (behind a flag, capture 👍/👎)" if rate >= bar else \
           "DO NOT SHIP — iterate prompt/model until eval clears the bar"


if __name__ == "__main__":
    feats = [
        Feature("Invoice total extraction", reach=5000, impact=2, confidence=0.8, effort=2),
        Feature("Full document chat",       reach=8000, impact=1, confidence=0.4, effort=8),
        Feature("Auto-categorize vendors",  reach=3000, impact=0.5, confidence=0.6, effort=1),
    ]
    print("RICE ranking (build the top one first):")
    for f in prioritize(feats):
        print(f"  {f.rice:7.1f}  {f.name}")

    rate = eval_pass_rate(fake_model)
    print(f"\nEval pass rate for top feature: {rate:.0%}")
    print("Decision:", ship_decision(rate))
```

Running it ranks invoice-extraction first (high confidence, low effort), then
the eval **fails the comma-decimal case** (75% < 85% bar) — so the disciplined
answer is *don't ship yet*. That is product sense made executable: prioritize by
value, then refuse to ship on hope.

## 6. How to learn it

**Beginner (weeks):**
- Read *The Mom Test* (customer discovery) and *Inspired* (Cagan).
- Ship one tiny thing end-to-end: a Gradio/Streamlit app deployed to HF Spaces
  with a 👍/👎 button. Milestone: a stranger uses it and you read the feedback.
- Write a one-page PRD for it *after* — problem, user, success metric, non-goals.

**Job-ready (months):**
- Build a real AI feature with: an eval set of 20+ real inputs, a feature flag,
  basic analytics (PostHog), and a North-Star metric you track weekly.
- Run one A/B test (two prompts/models) and make a ship/kill call from the data.
- Practice scoping: take a vague ask ("AI for our support team") and produce an
  MVP spec with explicit non-goals in under an hour. Milestone: you can defend
  *why you cut* a feature, not just what you built.

**Expert:**
- Own a roadmap: sequence features by RICE/WSJF, kill your own darlings, and tie
  every release to a metric movement you can attribute.
- Develop *taste* for the jagged frontier — predict where an LLM will fail before
  building, and design around it (HITL, fallbacks, narrowing).
- Mentor: review others' specs and reliably spot the missing non-goals, the
  untested riskiest assumption, and the missing kill switch.

## 7. Interview signals

What's probed: scoping judgment, prioritization rigor, bias-to-ship, and
learning from real usage. FDE/startup loops almost always include a *product
case*.

**Q: "We have an LLM. The support team wants 'AI to help with tickets.' Scope an
MVP."**
Strong answer: clarify the *job* (deflect tickets? speed up agents? draft
replies?), pick the one with highest value and lowest trust-risk (drafting
replies *for a human to send* — HITL de-risks trust), define a North-Star
(accepted-draft rate), name non-goals (no auto-send v1, English-only, top-3 ticket
types only), and a 2-week plan: 20-example eval first, then a flagged rollout to
5 agents. Show you'd *cut* full automation and multilingual to ship.

**Q: "Your demo wows the CEO but adoption is flat after launch. What do you do?"**
Strong answer: instrument the funnel (activation vs retention), watch session
replays / read 👎 feedback, form a hypothesis (likely: trust or it doesn't fit
the real workflow), test the cheapest fix, and be willing to conclude the *job*
was wrong. Mention that demo-wow ≠ retained value.

**Q: "How do you decide a feature is good enough to ship?"**
Strong answer: a pre-committed quality bar on a real eval set (#07), behind a
flag, with feedback capture and a kill switch — then ship and let real usage,
not opinion, drive the next iteration. Perfection is a learning tax.

**Red flags interviewers watch for:** building before validating demand,
no concept of non-goals, treating the model's quality as the only quality, and
inability to name a metric.
