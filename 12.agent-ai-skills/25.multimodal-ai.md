> **Scope (from the ranked list):** vision-language (CLIP, LLaVA, Qwen-VL,
> GPT-4V), speech (Whisper), image/video generation (diffusion), and cross-modal
> retrieval. *~25% and rising* (Meta S7, Microsoft S12). See
> [`../11.agent-ai-engineer/03.ranked-skills.md`](../11.agent-ai-engineer/03.ranked-skills.md).

## 1. What it is

**Multimodal AI** is building systems that work across *more than one* modality —
text, images, audio, video — either understanding them (a model that reads a
screenshot and answers questions) or generating them (text → image, text → speech).
The skill spans **vision-language models** (VLMs), **speech** (ASR/TTS),
**generative diffusion** for images/video, and **cross-modal retrieval** (search
images by text and vice-versa).

It exists because the world isn't text. Real product surfaces are documents,
screenshots, charts, photos, audio calls, and video — and users expect AI to
handle them natively. Most LLM engineers are *text-only*, so multimodal competence
is both increasingly *required* (document AI, voice agents, visual QA) and a
strong differentiator. The unifying idea that made it tractable: **everything can
be embedded into a shared vector space, and a transformer can consume tokens from
any modality.**

## 2. Why employers want it

Section 11 ranks this #25 and notes it's *rising*, named at Meta (S7) and
Microsoft (S12). The signal:

- **You unlock new product surfaces.** Document understanding, screenshot agents,
  voice interfaces, visual search, image generation — features text-only engineers
  can't build.
- **You understand cross-modal alignment.** You know how CLIP-style contrastive
  training aligns images and text, why it powers both retrieval and generation,
  and how VLMs glue a vision encoder to an LLM.
- **You handle the practical mess.** Image preprocessing, audio chunking, OCR vs
  native vision, token costs of high-res images, and eval for generative output.
- **You're scarce.** The pool of engineers fluent in *both* LLMs and vision/speech
  is small relative to demand.

## 3. Mental model

**The unifying trick: project every modality into a sequence of tokens/embeddings
a transformer can attend over.**

```
  IMAGE ─► vision encoder (ViT) ─► patch embeddings ─┐
  AUDIO ─► audio encoder ─────────► frame embeddings ─┼─► shared/projected space
  TEXT  ─► tokenizer + embed ─────► token embeddings ─┘
                                                       │
                              ┌─ CONTRASTIVE (CLIP): pull matched (img,text) together,
                              │   push mismatched apart → one space → retrieval, zero-shot
                              │
                              └─ FUSED (VLM): vision tokens are PREPENDED to text tokens,
                                  an LLM attends over both → "describe this image"
```

**Core architectures, in words:**

- **CLIP (contrastive).** Two encoders (image, text) trained so the embedding of
  an image and its caption have high cosine similarity, and mismatched pairs low.
  Result: a *shared* space enabling zero-shot classification ("is this a cat or a
  dog?" via text prompts), cross-modal retrieval, and the text-conditioning that
  guides diffusion. The InfoNCE loss is the workhorse.
- **VLMs (vision + LLM).** A *vision encoder* (often a CLIP ViT) turns an image
  into patch embeddings; a *projector* (MLP or cross-attention/Q-Former) maps them
  into the LLM's token space; the LLM consumes `[image tokens] + [text tokens]`
  and generates text. LLaVA, Qwen-VL, GPT-4V, Claude, Gemini, Llama-Vision all
  follow this shape. High-res images → *many* tokens → cost.
- **Diffusion (generation).** Learn to *denoise*: gradually add Gaussian noise to
  an image, then train a U-Net/DiT to reverse it step by step. Conditioning (text
  via CLIP/T5 embeddings, cross-attention) steers what's generated. **Latent
  diffusion** (Stable Diffusion) runs this in a compressed VAE latent space for
  speed. → see [`../03.generative-models/diffusion`](../03.generative-models/diffusion).
- **Speech.** **ASR** (Whisper): audio → log-mel spectrogram → encoder-decoder
  transformer → text. **TTS** (VITS, XTTS, Bark): text → audio. Increasingly
  *speech-native* LLMs handle audio tokens directly.
- **Video** = images over time; adds temporal modeling (3D attention) and huge
  compute.

**Practical concepts:** image tokenization cost (a 1024² image can be 1000+
tokens), OCR-vs-native-vision tradeoff for documents, audio chunking for long
files, classifier-free guidance (the knob that trades fidelity vs prompt-adherence
in diffusion), and that **generative eval is hard** (FID/CLIPScore for images, WER
for ASR, human preference for the rest).

## 4. The landscape

**Vision-language understanding (VLMs)**
- Open: **LLaVA / LLaVA-NeXT**, **Qwen2.5-VL**, **InternVL**, **Llama-3.2-Vision**,
  **Phi-3.5-Vision**, **Molmo**, **Pixtral**, **PaliGemma**. Closed: GPT-4o,
  Claude, Gemini. Use cases: visual QA, document/chart understanding, UI/screenshot
  agents, captioning.

**Contrastive / embedding (retrieval, zero-shot)**
- **CLIP** (OpenAI), **OpenCLIP** (open reproductions), **SigLIP** (Google, sigmoid
  loss — strong), **ALIGN**, **EVA-CLIP**. Power image search, dedup, zero-shot
  classification, and diffusion conditioning.

**Image/video generation**
- **Stable Diffusion / SDXL / SD3** (latent diffusion, DiT), **FLUX** (Black
  Forest Labs), **Imagen/Ideogram**, **Midjourney/DALL·E** (closed). Video:
  **Sora**, **Veo**, **Stable Video Diffusion**, **CogVideoX**, **Mochi**. Tooling:
  HF **Diffusers**, **ComfyUI**, ControlNet/IP-Adapter/LoRA for control. →
  [`../09.huggingface/diffusers`](../09.huggingface/diffusers).

**Speech**
- ASR: **Whisper** (OpenAI), **faster-whisper**, **NVIDIA Canary/Parakeet**,
  **wav2vec2**. TTS: **XTTS/Coqui**, **VITS**, **Bark**, **StyleTTS2**,
  **ElevenLabs** (closed). Real-time voice: GPT-4o voice, pipeline stacks
  (VAD → ASR → LLM → TTS).

**Document AI:** Donut, LayoutLMv3, native-vision VLMs, plus OCR (Tesseract,
PaddleOCR, Surya) — and the recurring decision: OCR-then-LLM vs feed-the-image.

**Tradeoffs:** native VLM vision is simpler and handles layout/figures, but costs
many tokens and can hallucinate text; OCR+LLM is cheaper/precise on dense text but
loses layout. CLIP-style retrieval is cheap and zero-shot but coarse; a VLM
reasons but is slow. Diffusion gives controllable generation but needs guidance
tuning and is compute-heavy. Pick by latency/cost/quality (the #17 triangle).

## 5. Worked example

The foundational multimodal primitive: **CLIP zero-shot classification and
cross-modal similarity** — embed an image and candidate text labels into the
*same* space and pick the closest. Uses open-CLIP via HF; downloads a small model
once, then runs on CPU.

```python
"""clip_zeroshot.py — CLIP puts images and text in ONE space (zero-shot + retrieval).
Run: pip install transformers torch pillow ; python clip_zeroshot.py
"""
import torch
from PIL import Image
from transformers import CLIPModel, CLIPProcessor

model = CLIPModel.from_pretrained("openai/clip-vit-base-patch32")
proc = CLIPProcessor.from_pretrained("openai/clip-vit-base-patch32")
model.eval()

# Make a synthetic image so this runs with no download of test data.
img = Image.new("RGB", (224, 224), color=(30, 120, 220))  # a blue square

# Zero-shot: we never trained on these labels — text prompts ARE the classifier.
labels = ["a photo of a blue square", "a photo of a red circle", "a cat"]

inputs = proc(text=labels, images=img, return_tensors="pt", padding=True)
with torch.no_grad():
    out = model(**inputs)
    # logits_per_image = image·text similarities (scaled by a learned temperature)
    probs = out.logits_per_image.softmax(dim=-1)[0]

for label, p in sorted(zip(labels, probs.tolist()), key=lambda x: -x[1]):
    print(f"  {p:6.2%}  {label}")

# The SAME embeddings power cross-modal RETRIEVAL: encode a corpus of images and
# rank them by cosine similarity to a TEXT query (or vice-versa) — that's how
# "search my photos by description" and diffusion text-conditioning both work.
with torch.no_grad():
    img_emb = model.get_image_features(pixel_values=inputs["pixel_values"])
    txt_emb = model.get_text_features(
        input_ids=inputs["input_ids"], attention_mask=inputs["attention_mask"])
img_emb = img_emb / img_emb.norm(dim=-1, keepdim=True)   # L2-normalize → cosine
txt_emb = txt_emb / txt_emb.norm(dim=-1, keepdim=True)
print("\ncosine(image, 'blue square') =",
      (img_emb @ txt_emb[0]).item().__round__(3))
```

This one model demonstrates the whole multimodal foundation: a *shared embedding
space* gives you zero-shot classification (no labels needed — prompts are the
classes), cross-modal retrieval (the same vectors rank images against text), and
the text-conditioning signal that steers diffusion generators. VLMs extend it by
feeding those image features *into* an LLM.

## 6. How to learn it

**Beginner (weeks).** Use CLIP for zero-shot classification and image↔text
retrieval (above); transcribe audio with Whisper; generate an image with Diffusers
(SDXL/FLUX). Understand patches, spectrograms, and the denoising idea. *Milestone:*
a small app that captions or searches images, or transcribes speech.

**Job-ready (1–3 months).** Run a VLM (LLaVA/Qwen-VL) for visual QA and document
understanding; build a multimodal RAG (embed images + text, retrieve across both);
fine-tune a CLIP or a LoRA on a diffusion model; build a voice pipeline (VAD → ASR
→ LLM → TTS). Learn the token-cost and OCR-vs-vision tradeoffs. *Milestone:* a
deployed multimodal feature (document QA or visual search or a voice agent) with
real inputs.

**Expert.** Understand VLM architecture deeply (vision encoder + projector +
LLM; high-res tiling; any-resolution handling); train/adapt diffusion (ControlNet,
LoRA, DPO for diffusion); design generative evaluation (FID, CLIPScore, human
preference, WER); optimize multimodal inference (image-token budgets, caching).
*Milestone:* you architect and ship a production multimodal system and can defend
its quality/cost.

## 7. Interview signals

What's probed: do you understand *how* modalities are unified, and the practical
tradeoffs?

**Q: "How does CLIP enable zero-shot image classification?"**
Strong answer: it trains image and text encoders contrastively (InfoNCE) so a
matched image-caption pair has high cosine similarity in a *shared* space. At
inference you embed the image and a set of text prompts ("a photo of a {label}")
and pick the highest-similarity label — no labeled training for those classes.
Same embeddings give cross-modal retrieval and diffusion conditioning.

**Q: "How does a vision-language model 'see' an image?"**
Strong answer: a vision encoder (a ViT, often CLIP's) splits the image into
patches and produces patch embeddings; a projector (MLP or Q-Former/cross-
attention) maps them into the LLM's token space; the LLM attends over `[image
tokens] + [text tokens]` and generates. High resolution → many image tokens → more
cost, the main practical constraint.

**Q: "Explain how diffusion generates an image."**
Strong answer: train a network to *reverse* a noising process — start from pure
noise and iteratively denoise toward an image, conditioned on a text embedding via
cross-attention. Latent diffusion (Stable Diffusion) does this in a compressed VAE
latent space for speed; classifier-free guidance trades fidelity vs prompt
adherence.

**Q: "Document QA: feed the image to a VLM, or OCR then LLM?"**
Strong answer: depends. Native VLM handles layout, figures, and charts and is
simpler, but costs many tokens and can misread dense text. OCR+LLM is cheaper and
precise on text-heavy pages but loses spatial layout. Often hybrid (OCR for text,
vision for figures). Decide on the cost/latency/accuracy triangle for the doc type.
