# 03 · The GPT line — GPT-1 → 4o and the reasoning models (o1/o3)

> **Intuition.** Take a Transformer *decoder*, mask attention so each token sees
> only the past, and train it on one objective: **predict the next token**. That
> single objective, scaled up enough, turns into translation, coding, reasoning,
> and conversation — capabilities *nobody trained for explicitly*. The GPT line
> is the story of riding that scaling curve and then aligning the result.

> **Public vs inferred.** GPT-1/2 are fully open (weights + paper). GPT-3's
> architecture is published but weights are closed. From **GPT-3.5 onward,
> architecture, size, and data are largely undisclosed** — anything specific
> below is flagged **(inferred)** and reflects credible community estimates, not
> OpenAI confirmation.

---

## 1. The shared backbone

Every GPT is a **decoder-only** Transformer: token + position embeddings → `L`
blocks of {causal multi-head self-attention → FFN}, each wrapped in residuals
and **pre-LayerNorm** (GPT-2 onward), → tied output projection → softmax over a
**BPE** vocabulary. Training loss is plain autoregressive cross-entropy:

$$\mathcal{L} = -\sum_{t} \log p_\theta(x_t \mid x_{<t})$$

Inference is autoregressive sampling with a **KV cache** (`08.kv_cache.py`) so
each new token reuses past keys/values instead of recomputing them.

## 2. The evolution

### GPT-1 (2018, 117M)
*Generative pre-training then discriminative fine-tuning.* Showed that
unsupervised LM pretraining + a small task head beats training from scratch.
12 layers, learned positions, BookCorpus.

### GPT-2 (2019, 124M → 1.5B)
Same recipe, **10× bigger**, trained on WebText (40GB). Moved LayerNorm to the
input of each sub-block (**pre-norm**) and added a final LN. The headline: strong
**zero-shot** task transfer with no fine-tuning — "language models are
unsupervised multitask learners." Staged release over misuse concerns.

### GPT-3 (2020, up to 175B)
96 layers, 12,288 hidden, 2048 context, alternating dense/locally-banded
attention. The discovery: **in-context / few-shot learning** — show examples in
the prompt and the frozen model adapts, no gradient updates. Scale alone unlocked
it. This is the paper that made "just make it bigger" the industry strategy
(see Kaplan scaling laws, `11.training-and-alignment.md`).

### GPT-3.5 / InstructGPT (2022)
The pivot from "raw LM" to "assistant." **RLHF** (Reinforcement Learning from
Human Feedback): (1) supervised fine-tune on human demonstrations, (2) train a
**reward model** on human preference rankings, (3) optimize the policy against
the reward with **PPO** (+ a KL penalty to stay near the SFT model). InstructGPT
1.3B was preferred over raw GPT-3 175B — *alignment beat raw scale* for
usefulness. ChatGPT (Nov 2022) productized this. (See
`11.training-and-alignment.md`.)

### GPT-4 (2023)
Multimodal (text + image in, text out), large-scale RLHF, much stronger
reasoning, 8k/32k then 128k context. **Architecture and size undisclosed.**
Widely *speculated* (inferred) to be a **Mixture-of-Experts** (~8 or 16 experts)
in the ~1.8T-total range — **not confirmed by OpenAI**. Treat all GPT-4 internals
as unofficial.

### GPT-4o (2024) — "omni"
A single model natively handling **text, audio, and image** in and out, much
faster and cheaper than GPT-4-Turbo, real-time voice. Native multimodal tokens
rather than bolted-on adapters. Internals undisclosed **(inferred)**.

### The reasoning models — o1 (2024), o3 / o3-mini / o4-mini (2024–25)
A new axis: **inference-time compute.** These models are trained with
**reinforcement learning to produce a long internal chain-of-thought** before
answering — they "think," exploring and backtracking, then emit a concise final
answer (the private reasoning is hidden from users). Spending more *test-time*
tokens reliably raises accuracy on math, code, and science (AIME, Codeforces,
GPQA jumps). o1 preview Sept 2024, full Dec 2024; o3 announced Dec 2024, o3-mini
Jan 2025, o3 + o4-mini Apr 2025; o3-pro June 2025. Exact architectures
undisclosed **(inferred to share the GPT-4-class backbone + RL post-training)**.

## 4. Mechanisms & component links

GPT-class models popularized — and now use — much of this folder's stack:
- **KV cache** for fast decoding — `08.kv_cache.py`.
- **FlashAttention** for memory-efficient training/inference — `09.flash_attention.py`.
- **Speculative decoding** to accelerate generation with a draft model —
  `11.speculative_decoding.py` (relevant to fast serving of these models).
- The **(inferred)** GPT-4 MoE would use sparse routing — `07.moe.py`.
- **Quantization** for serving — `15.quantization.py`.
- RLHF/DPO alignment is detailed in `11.training-and-alignment.md`.

## 5. Strengths / limits

Decoder-only + next-token + scale is astonishingly general and simple to train
and serve. Limits: quadratic attention (mitigated by Flash/sliding-window),
hallucination, and the closed nature of the frontier models — you must take their
architecture claims on trust. The reasoning models trade **latency and cost** for
accuracy, a genuinely new operating point.

See `12.references.md` for the GPT-1/2/3, InstructGPT, GPT-4 system card, GPT-4o,
and o1/o3 sources.
